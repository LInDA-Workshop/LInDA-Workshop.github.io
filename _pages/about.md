<p><i>LInDA studies how linguistic information can improve downstream NLP applications.</i></p>
<p>Large language models (LLMs) still perform poorly on many languages. Most of the world's languages lack large training corpora. However, many of them have other resources: dictionaries, grammars, morphological analyses, and other forms of annotated texts. For example, according to Glottolog, about 63% of the world's languages have at least a grammar sketch.</p>
<p>Recent work shows that this kind of information can help NLP systems. For example, LLMs can translate a language they have never seen before when they receive a dictionary and a grammar book in the prompt. Typological descriptions and interlinear glosses also may help machine translation and other tasks.</p>
<p>LInDA brings these two sides together: linguistic information and NLP applications. We want to understand how linguistic resources and annotations can improve downstream tasks, especially for low-resource and underrepresented languages. We also want to know which types of information are useful, and for which tasks.</p>
<p>The workshop has two goals:</p>
<ol><li>To evaluate strategies that integrate linguistic information into AI systems, and to measure their impact on specific applications.</li>
<li>To explore how AI models can help create or extract linguistic information.</li></ol>
{% comment %}
<p>Other workshops, such as SIGTYP, SIGMORPHON, MRL, or LoResMT, cover parts of this space. LInDA focuses on the link between the two. For this reason, every submission must include both parts: a linguistic resource or annotation, and its effect on at least one NLP task.</p>
{% endcomment %}
<p>We welcome researchers with a background in linguistics, in computer science, or in both. This is the first edition of LInDA. It will be co-located with EACL 2027 in Athens, Greece, and will run in a hybrid format.</p>
